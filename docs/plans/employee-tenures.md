# Employee Tenures and Historical HR Numbers

Status: Approved
Date: 2026-11-15

## Goal and confirmed decisions
One persistent employee, multiple recruitment tenures, one HR number per tenure shared across employers. User confirmed: only one effective HR number per person at a time; rehire may reuse the previous number or choose a new number; a newly linked employee account sees only its current tenure; preserve all existing data. HR access remains permission/department scoped, not unrestricted.

## Minimal model and invariants
- Keep `employees.id`, profiles, relationship IDs, segment IDs, contracts and historical foreign keys unchanged. Existing ADR defines `employments` as assignment segments, not recruitment tenures (`docs/decisions/2026-11-12-employment-relationship-and-contract-boundaries.md:25`).
- Add `employee_tenures(id, employee_id, employee_number, start_date, end_date, user_id)` with standard timestamps; add `employment_relationships.tenure_id`. Derive segment tenure through relationship, avoiding redundant links where possible. Do not reinterpret existing employment IDs.
- Store number as text; trim/normalize consistently without losing leading zeros. A number may recur across closed and new tenures for the same person, but must not identify two different people in overlapping effective periods; enforce non-overlapping tenure date ranges per person and at most one open tenure with PostgreSQL constraints. A relationship must belong to the same employee and fit its tenure dates; segments must fit their relationship. Different employers may overlap within one tenure.
- Tenure closure is explicit. Ending one employer relationship must not end the tenure while another remains effective. Transfers preserve tenure/number. Rehire requires a closed previous tenure and may choose either the old number or a new number; dates cannot overlap. No hard deletion or generic profile-edit bypass for tenure identity changes. HRM never equates employee number with an AD username.
- Account link belongs to tenure; it may initially be null. Employee ESS authorization requires the authenticated user's current effective tenure plus object membership, never employee UUID alone. Historical actor IDs remain unchanged. Revocation concerns HRM only; AD provisioning/deprovisioning remains external.

## Test impact / acceptance
Success: same person and number across employers A/B; close tenure, rehire with reused or new number, no duplicate person. Regression: segment/contract IDs, finalized payroll and signed documents unchanged; old reports keep old numbers. Boundaries: blank/duplicate/leading-zero codes, invalid UUID/date, overlap, future dates, multiple employers, unresolved legacy data. State/failure: double submit, concurrent rehire/closure, transaction rollback, DB/cancellation/revocation failures. Security: old sessions and cross-tenure IDs cannot read old ESS data; HR respects RBAC. Compatibility: old requests fail explicitly or follow documented safe adapter; mixed versions cannot corrupt history. Operations: masked audit, bounded migration/search, no N+1, recovery drill.
